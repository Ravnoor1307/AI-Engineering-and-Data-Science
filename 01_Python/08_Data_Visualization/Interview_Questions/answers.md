# Data Visualization - model answers

Crisp answers matched to [`questions.md`](questions.md). Say the first sentence in an interview, then expand only if asked.

## Easy

**1. What chart shows a distribution?**

A histogram or KDE for one variable; a box or violin plot when comparing distributions across categories.

**2. When do you use a bar chart versus a line chart?**

Bars compare discrete categories; lines show change across an ordered continuum, usually time.

**3. What is wrong with pie charts?**

Humans compare angles poorly. A sorted bar chart conveys the same proportions more accurately, especially beyond three slices.

**4. What is the difference between a figure and an axes?**

The figure is the whole canvas; an axes is one plotting area within it, with its own data, ticks and labels.

**5. Why label axes?**

Without names and units a chart cannot be interpreted or audited. It is the cheapest credibility you can add.

**6. What is a scatter plot for?**

Showing the relationship between two numeric variables, including its shape, strength and outliers.

**7. What does alpha control?**

Opacity. Lowering it reveals density where markers overlap.

**8. How do you save a matplotlib figure?**

`fig.savefig(path, dpi=300, bbox_inches='tight')`, choosing PNG for raster and SVG or PDF for vector output.

**9. What is seaborn built on?**

Matplotlib. Seaborn adds DataFrame awareness, statistical estimation and better defaults.

**10. What is a heatmap useful for?**

Showing a matrix of values by colour, most commonly a correlation matrix or a two-way pivot table.

## Medium

**11. Explain figure-level versus axes-level seaborn functions.**

Axes-level functions draw onto an Axes you supply; figure-level functions such as relplot create and own their whole figure, enabling faceting.

**12. How do you choose a colour palette?**

Sequential for ordered magnitude, diverging for deviation around a centre, qualitative for unordered categories - and always check colour-blind safety.

**13. What is small multiples and when is it better than one busy chart?**

A grid of identically scaled panels, one per group. It beats overplotting once you have more than about four series.

**14. How do you visualise a million points?**

Aggregate first: 2-D histograms, hexbin, datashader, or sample. Drawing a million markers is both slow and unreadable.

**15. What is chart junk?**

Any ink that does not convey data: 3D effects, heavy gridlines, decorative backgrounds. Remove it to raise the data-ink ratio.

**16. Why should a bar chart's axis start at zero?**

Bar length encodes magnitude, so a truncated axis exaggerates differences. Line charts, which encode change, may be truncated with care.

**17. How do you show uncertainty?**

Error bars, confidence bands, or plotting the raw points alongside the summary. A bare mean hides the variance.

**18. What is the difference between px and graph_objects in plotly?**

Plotly Express is the concise high-level API; graph_objects gives full control over every trace and layout property.

**19. How do you make charts accessible?**

Colour-blind safe palettes, redundant encoding with shape or labels, sufficient contrast, and readable font sizes.

**20. When is a log scale appropriate?**

When data spans orders of magnitude or when you want to read multiplicative growth as a straight line. Always label it clearly.

## Hard

**21. Design a dashboard for an executive audience.**

Lead with three or four headline metrics and their trend, then supporting breakdowns. One question per chart, consistent scales, explicit time window, and a stated data freshness timestamp.

**22. How do you evaluate whether a visualisation is effective?**

Test it: can a target reader answer the intended question in under ten seconds without help? Effectiveness is measured on the reader, not the author.

**23. Explain perceptual ranking of visual encodings.**

Position is read most accurately, then length, angle, area, and colour saturation last. Encode the most important variable with position.

**24. How do you handle time zones in time series charts?**

Store UTC, convert once to the business timezone for display, label the timezone on the axis, and beware DST-shortened days in daily aggregates.

**25. What is Simpson's paradox and how can a chart reveal it?**

An aggregate trend that reverses within subgroups. Faceting or colouring by the confounder exposes it; a single pooled line hides it.

**26. How would you build a reproducible figure pipeline?**

Figures generated by scripts from versioned data, parameters in a config file, deterministic seeds, and output written to a tracked artefacts folder.

**27. Discuss interactive versus static trade-offs.**

Interactive helps exploration and stakeholder self-service but adds weight, dependencies and accessibility burden. Static is reproducible, printable and diffable.

**28. How do you visualise high-dimensional data?**

Reduce with PCA, t-SNE or UMAP for structure, use parallel coordinates for moderate dimensionality, and always validate that the projection is not creating artificial clusters.

**29. What are the pitfalls of dual-axis charts?**

The relative scaling is arbitrary, so apparent correlation can be manufactured. Prefer indexed series or two stacked panels.

**30. How do you choose bin width for a histogram?**

Start with a rule such as Freedman-Diaconis, then check several widths. Bin choice can hide or invent modes, so never rely on one.
